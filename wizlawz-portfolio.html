How he thinks

Definition-first and evidence-bound. Observe what exists. Preserve what occurs. Distinguish observation from interpretation. Challenge assumptions. Verify through evidence. Amend when necessary — and retain the history of every change.

He has maintained a single symbolic framework — an A–Z codex — for thirty years, and he builds by pattern: he finds the shape a system is already following, across domains that are not usually in the same room, and works from the shape rather than from convention. His thinking spans three areas at once — cognitive systems (how intelligence processes, learns, adapts, and reasons), technical systems (how those processes become functioning, testable architectures), and human interaction (how people and artificial intelligence communicate, cooperate, and develop together). That span is not a theory with him. It is his daily working practice.
